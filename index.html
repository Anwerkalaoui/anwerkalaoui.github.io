SCENE 00 — WHO AM I
Anwer Kalaoui — Tunis

I am Anwer Kalaoui, a filmmaker and videographer. My journey began in legal studies and then shifted toward cinema, where I pursued a Master's degree at the Higher School of Cinema (ESAC). This transition from law to film continues to shape my practice, sharpening my attention to the structures, rules, and invisible systems that govern our lives.

Cinema, for me, is not only a narrative tool but a space of inquiry. My fascination with cinematic language has evolved into a way of questioning reality, exploring how power, memory, and emotion circulate between the intimate and the collective. Through images and sound, I seek to create experiences that move between sensation and reflection, where cinema becomes a site of tension, perception, and thought.

“The cinema is not an art which films life: the cinema is something between art and life.”

Six bodies of work moving between portraiture, documentary and the visual language of protest — held together by a recurring question: what shapes us before we choose anything.

Melancholy

Photo Story
Tunisia

A poetic photo story about a girl haunted by a silent presence — a ghost dressed in black. Through moments of solitude, reflection and absence, she walks the edge between darkness and light. Each image captures her inner struggle between despair and hope.

Power Surrounds Us

Ongoing Series
Tunisia

A red circle intrudes on frame after frame — a lens flare from an unseen authority. Traced through Tunis's streets and skylines, the series follows how systems of control quietly shape everyday geometry, and the people trying to move through it.

06

Effervescence

2025
Documentary Essay · Writer, Director & Editor

Effervescence is an experimental documentary that explores the existential journey of a young man born into a life he never chose — his name, family, language and social background all determined before he had the chance to decide for himself. Beginning with an intimate family story, the film gradually unfolds into a reflection on identity, freedom and memory. Through observational footage, poetic voice-over and a fragmented narrative, it blurs the line between documentary and cinematic essay, transforming everyday moments into a space for existential inquiry.
